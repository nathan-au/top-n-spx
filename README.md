# top-n-spx

Backtests a simple strategy: each year, equal-weight the top N S&P 500
companies by market cap (as ranked at the *end of the prior year*), hold for
12 months, rebalance. Compares the result against the S&P 500 total-return
index over 2000-2025, for every N from 1 to 20.

## How it works

1. At the start of year `Y`, look up the market-cap ranking as of the end of
   year `Y-1` and take the top `N` tickers.
2. Buy those tickers equal-weighted at the last close of `Y-1`, hold through
   the last close of `Y`.
3. Repeat for every year in the backtest range, sweep `N` from 1 to 20, and
   compare each N's annual returns, Sharpe, Sortino, and max drawdown against
   the S&P 500 total-return index.

Only information known at the time a position is opened is used — the
ranking that decides year `Y`'s holdings always comes from `Y-1`, never from
`Y` itself.

## Data

`data/` is gitignored and not shipped with the repo. Two kinds of inputs are
needed:

**1. Manually-provided reference data** (not generated by anything in this
repo — you need to supply these three files yourself before running
anything):

- `data/top-20-spx-companies-by-market-cap-by-year.json` — for each year,
  the top 20 S&P 500 companies by market cap (rank -> `{name, ticker,
  market_cap}`). This is the backtest's source of truth for what was "big"
  in a given year, and it's known to be current only as of the date it was
  compiled (see the comment at the top of `data.py`). If you regenerate or
  replace it, verify it's a genuine point-in-time snapshot and not a
  hindsight reconstruction — see **Caveats** below.
- `data/top-20-vs-spx-market-cap-by-year.json` — top-20 vs. total S&P 500
  market cap by year, used only by `concentration.py`.
- `data/fama-french-risk-free-rates-by-year.json` — annual risk-free rate by
  year, used for Sharpe/Sortino excess-return calculations.

**2. Downloaded price data** (generated by `data.py` via `yfinance`, once the
files above are in place):

```bash
python3 data.py
```

This fetches and caches, per year, under `data/spx/` and `data/held/`:

- `data/spx/<year>-1mo.csv` — the S&P 500 **total-return** index
  (`^SP500TR`), monthly closes. Total return, not price-only, because the
  held stocks are fetched with dividends reinvested (`auto_adjust=True`) —
  the benchmark has to match or every comparison overstates the strategy.
- `data/held/<year>-1mo.csv` — monthly closes (dividend-adjusted) for the
  prior year's top-20 tickers, spanning prior-December through the target
  year's December, i.e. exactly the purchase-to-sale window the backtest
  needs.

Re-running `data.py` skips years already cached; pass `overwrite=True` to
`download_and_save_data` / `download_and_save_held_data` to force a refetch.

## Running the backtest

```bash
python3 main.py
```

This runs the strategy for `top_n` = 1..20 over 2000-2025, prints a metrics
table per N, then a return/drawdown scatter with the Pareto-optimal frontier
across the swept N values.

`concentration.py` is a separate, standalone script — it just plots how much
of the S&P 500's total market cap the top 20 companies have represented over
time.

## Results (2000-2025)

| top_n | Total Return | Sharpe | Max Drawdown |
|------:|-------------:|-------:|--------------:|
| 1     | 783%         | 0.37   | -27.9%        |
| 2     | 979%         | 0.40   | -34.2%        |
| 5     | 827%         | 0.39   | -41.6%        |
| 10    | 621%         | 0.39   | -35.2%        |
| 15    | 766%         | 0.43   | -34.5%        |
| 19    | 774%         | **0.45** | -31.5%      |
| 20    | 732%         | 0.44   | -34.8%        |
| **SPX (total return)** | **653%** | **0.43** | **-37.0%** |

13 of the 20 tested values of N have a **lower** Sharpe than the S&P 500
itself; only the N in the high teens is even marginally ahead, and by less
than the sampling noise in a 26-point annual series (see Caveats).

Splitting the sample tells the real story:

- **2000-2016**: every N *underperforms* the index, often badly (N=1: +4%
  vs. the index's +112%; N=20: +80% vs. +112%).
- **2017-2025**: every N outperforms (N=2: +830% vs. the index's +256%), but
  Sharpe is a near-tie with the index (0.93 vs. 0.86 at best) — this is the
  Mag-7 / mega-cap-tech era, not a distinct edge from "bigness" itself.

**Conclusion: this is not a validated strategy.** The apparent full-sample
outperformance comes almost entirely from one nine-year bull market in
mega-cap tech, not from a persistent mechanism. For 17 of the last 26 years,
buying the biggest S&P names would have lagged just holding the index.

## Caveats

- **Survivorship risk in the ranking data.** None of Enron, WorldCom,
  Lucent, Sun Microsystems, Yahoo, or AOL appear anywhere in the top-20
  rankings, despite several of them genuinely being top-10-by-market-cap S&P
  names around 1999-2001 before collapsing to zero. If the source list only
  tracks companies traceable by a still-valid ticker today, it silently
  drops the losers — which can only inflate every result above, never
  deflate it. This hasn't been corrected for.
- **Small sample.** 26 non-overlapping annual observations. The standard
  error on an annual Sharpe estimate here is roughly 0.2 — bigger than most
  of the Sharpe differences in the table above.
- **In-sample N selection.** Sweeping N and picking the best one from the
  same backtest it's evaluated on is overfitting; adjacent N values share
  most of their holdings, so the 20 "strategies" are really one strategy
  sampled at slightly different points, not 20 independent tests.
- **No transaction costs, taxes, or slippage.** Annual reconstitution,
  especially at small N, can mean near-total portfolio turnover in a year.
- **Drawdown is computed from year-end snapshots only**, even though monthly
  data is already downloaded — true intra-year drawdowns (e.g. Feb-Mar 2020)
  aren't captured.
- **Equal-weighted, not cap-weighted.** The strategy tests "own the current
  mega-caps equally," not "own the index's actual concentration" — a
  related but different bet.
